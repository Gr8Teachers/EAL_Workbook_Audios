Show transcript – Single-use plastics

Single-use plastics in Australia. What is single-use plastic?   

These are plastic products that are made to be used only once. They should be recycled, but in Australia, only 12% of plastics are recycled. We use too many single-use plastics, and we don’t recycle enough. A lot of plastic ends up in our parks, nature and oceans. This is not good.  

Most states and territories in Australia are banning single-use plastics, but they won’t be banned everywhere until at least 2025.  

In the meantime, you can help. You should change single-use plastic for something else.  

Here’s what you can do. 

Number 1. Plastic straws 

In Australia, nearly 1.3 billion plastic straws were used in 2020. They are light, so they get blown by the wind into our waterways and oceans. They’re dangerous for our marine wildlife. Thankfully, these are now banned in most Australian states and territories. They will be banned everywhere in Australia by 2025. Instead of plastic straws you can use stainless steel straws, bamboo straws or paper straws.   

Number 2. Balloons 

Helium balloons may be pretty, but they’re deadly for seabirds when they fall into the ocean. It is better not to use balloons at all. Try paper lanterns or flowers instead at your next party.  

Number 3. Plastic cotton buds 

Many cotton buds end up in our oceans. Once the cotton tip dissolves, all that’s left is a hard plastic stick. These can kill birds, fish and other marine wildlife when swallowed. Cotton buds have also been banned in many states and territories in Australia. Try using bamboo cotton buds, organic cotton makeup pads or a reusable pad.  

 Number 4. Coffee cups and lids 

Australians use around 1.8 billion take away coffee cups every year and 1.5 billion coffee lids. Most takeaway coffee cups and lids can’t be recycled or composted. They end up in landfill. Why not take your own reusable coffee mug next time you buy a cup of coffee? 

Number 5. Plastic cutlery 

When you get takeaway, you might get plastic cutlery with it. But in Australia, plastic cutlery isn’t easy to recycle. Recycling machines can’t sort them because of their shape, so many end up in landfill - maybe for decades or even centuries. Plastic cutlery is now banned in many Australian states and territories, but you might still be given plastic cutlery.   

Next time you order takeaway, say no to plastic cutlery. Try using reusable bamboo cutlery. Or have your own travel cutlery set. You could even bring your own from home! Chopsticks are also a great alternative to have in your bag if you’re planning to get takeaway.
