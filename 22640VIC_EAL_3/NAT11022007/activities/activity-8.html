Unstressed syllable

The schwa /Ə/ symbol is used to represent an unstressed syllable in a word.

Schwa /Ə/ is a shorter vowel sound and is important for creating the rhythm of spoken English. Schwa /Ə/ is quite difficult to hear and pronounce because it is not found in other languages. If you do not pronounce schwa /Ə/, all vowel sounds will have equal length and your speaking will sound monotonous.

For example, the word analysis has a weak syllable followed by a strong syllable stress on NAL followed by 2 weak syllables. The pronunciation symbols in the dictionary for the word analysis are /əˈnæl.ə.sɪs/. In this example, you can identify the syllable that is stressed following the /‘ / and 2 schwa /Ə/ syllables, one before and one after the stressed syllable. The final unstressed syllable is said softly.

Shifting syllable stress

Sometimes, when you change the stress in a word, it can change its meaning. There are English words that can be used as either a noun or a verb. Shifting the syllable can change the word from its noun form to its verb form, or vice-versa.

For example, the verb ‘educate’ is the first syllable is stressed and shown in bold ‘educate’. The related noun ‘education’ the second syllable is stressed ‘education’.

Often the first syllable is stressed when the word is a noun. The second syllable is stressed when the word is a verb. In this table you can see changing word stress.

Noun: stress on first syllable Verb: stress on second syllable
combine combine
conduct conduct
contest contest
