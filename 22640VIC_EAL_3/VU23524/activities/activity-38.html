Rising and falling intonation

Intonation is how we communicate meaning by changing the pitch of our voice combined with sentence stress and rhythm. Pitch means how high or low your voice is. Sentence stress means the words in a sentence which you say longer and louder. While rhythm is the beat of English speech.

When asking questions, the way we use our voice is important. We can use intonation and change the pitch of our voice to go higher or lower. Using intonation in questions makes us sound polite and friendly. This will help you speak in transactions.

To make our voice go higher, we use a rising intonation.

We use a rising intonation for questions that have a yes or no answer. In yes or no questions, begin with these question words:

  • do or does + base verb

  • did + base verb

  • is or are + noun or adjective

  • was or were + noun or adjective

  • has or have

  • can or could

  • would.

Falling intonation falls at the end of the question. When we ask ‘wh’ questions, the intonation rises at the beginning and falls at the end. Here are examples of rising and falling intonation used in a question. Ask your teacher if you require additional support.
