Show transcript – Lesson 1 – Casual conversation
Helen: Morning Joshua. How's it going?
Joshua: Oh, hi Helen. I'm alright. How about you?
Helen: Not bad... Hey, what a storm last night! How was that thunder? And it's still pouring this morning, too.
Joshua:

 Yeah, the thunder was quite loud. I don't usually let my dog in my room but he was so scared, he was whimpering in the bathroom. I ended up letting him into my room in the middle of the night.
Helen: Oh poor thing. And look at you. Goodness me! Your jacket's all wet!
Joshua:
 Tell me about it. I got absolutely soaked! [stress bold words, falling intonation for emphasis]
Helen: Yeah, I can see that. Didn't you have an umbrella?
Joshua:

 Actually, I did have one, but the rain was so heavy and the wind was so strong that I ended up getting wet anyway. And it didn't help that I had to walk from the train station to work either!
Helen: I bet.
Joshua: So how did you get to work?
Helen: I drove, like I always do.
Joshua: Well! No wonder you're not wet!
Helen:
 Yeah. [laughs] So can I ask you...why don't you drive? It would save you getting wet when the weather's like it is today.
Joshua:
 Well, to be honest, I prefer not to drive. [really stress the word in bold for emphasis]
Helen: Oh really?
Joshua: Yeah, I prefer to take public transport — even if it does mean getting drenched!
Helen: Mmm... Why is that?
Joshua: Well, I just reckon public transport is better for so many reasons. For starters, it's cheaper than having a car.
Helen:
 Do you think so? [rising intonation for this question, stress on the word in bold]
Joshua:

 Well...yeah... If you think about it, if you have a car, you have to pay for petrol, rego, insurance, even parking sometimes. And then if something goes wrong, or you get into an accident, that can be super expensive.
Helen:
 Yeah, true, but public transport isn't as cheap as it used to be. [stress words in bold for emphasis]
Joshua: I guess so, but I still think it would be cheaper than having a car.
Helen: Maybe... But don't you think having a car is way more convenient? Public transport can only take you to certain places at certain times.
Joshua: Yeah, I suppose so, but at least you don't need to find a parking spot! [stress words in bold for emphasis]
Helen: Oh, I know what you mean. Parking can be such a nightmare! [stress words in bold for emphasis]
Joshua:

 Definitely! So tell me... How do you cope with the traffic? It seems to be getting worse and worse, especially in the mornings. [stress word in bold and say it slowly for emphasis]
Helen:

 Well, that really depends on where you live. Around here, it's not too bad. The traffic's only heavy at certain times of the day. Peak-hour seems to be around 8 to 9 o'clock in the morning and 4 to 5 in the afternoon, so I try to avoid these times as much as I can.
Joshua: Yeah, I suppose it really does depend on where you live and what direction you're travelling in.
Helen:
 True... And I really do just love the freedom of going wherever I like — day or night — and not having to wait for a bus or a train to take me.
Joshua:

 Mmm... Well, I try to plan my trips as much as possible, so I look it up online and work out which trains or buses I need to catch to get to where I need to go. It takes a bit of organisation but I'm used to it now.
Helen:

 That's a good idea. But with my kids, I think I would find that very difficult to do. If they get sick or there's an emergency, or I have to take them somewhere and pick them up at specific times...waiting for public transport to arrive makes it too hard.
Joshua:

 Mmm... That's true. I'm living on my own at the moment. It's just me and my dog, so I suppose it is much easier for me. [laughs] Yeah, it really does depend on your lifestyle.
Helen:


 Yes, you're right. And look...even though parking and traffic jams can sometimes be a problem, I still really like the fact I can get exactly where I want to go... But on public transport, you know, sometimes the bus drops you off miles from where you're going...or there's no train station nearby. [stress word in bold for emphasis]
Joshua:

 Yeah, I get what you're saying. But you know what? I do like keeping fit... I see the walking as incidental exercise. [body language suggestion: use air quotes when saying the words in bold]
Helen: Huh? What do you mean by that? [body language: puzzled facial expression]
Joshua:


 So... I mean that every morning, I walk to work from the station and then back again in the afternoons — that's at least half an hour of exercise every day. That doesn't include the time I spend walking from my house to the station either. So I'm getting my physical exercise without planning a gym session or anything like that.
Helen: Yeah, walking is really good for you if you have the time. We need more trains and buses and stations that are closer together.
Joshua: [body language: nods] I agree 100%. In my opinion, the government really needs to invest more money into public transport in this city.
Helen: I know, right? We went to New York last year and their subway system is amazing. I don't think you'd need a car if you lived there!
Joshua: Totally! ...Anyway, how was New York? I don't think I've asked you about it.
Helen:

 Oh, we had the best time over there. The kids had a ball. They loved Central Park and the zoo. We went to a couple of great museums and we took them to see their favourite basketball team play. It was so much fun.
Joshua: Sounds fantastic!
Helen: Yeah, it was. I'll have to show you some photos.
Joshua: That would be great! Maybe at lunch? I have to go into a meeting now.
Helen: Oh yes, of course. Great chatting with you.
Joshua: Yes. See you at lunch.
